Local SEO for a single-location service business in San Antonio runs from about $100 a month for a tool you drive yourself, to $300 to $700 a month for a freelancer or a small shop, to $500 to $2,000 a month for an agency. One-time setups and audits run $500 to $5,000. Surveys of small businesses put most of them in the $100 to $500 a month range. None of those numbers tells you much on its own. What you get for them does.

## What are the price bands, and what should each include?

**$0 to $150 a month: do it yourself, maybe with a tool.** Your time plus, optionally, a listings or review tool. You should be doing the [Google Business Profile checklist](/learn/google-business-profile-checklist-san-antonio), asking for a review after every job, and checking your listings quarterly. This works if you'll actually do it. Most owners do it once and stop.

**$300 to $700 a month: a freelancer or a one-person shop.** You should get hands on your profile every month (posts, photos, Q&A), review asks sent and replies written, listings checked, a page or guide added every quarter, and a one-page report that shows calls, direction requests, website clicks, review count and map rank over time. You should know the person's name and reach them directly. This is where [Keep Ranking](/services/keep-ranking) sits, at $450.

**$800 to $2,000 a month: an agency.** On top of the above, you should get content writing, more pages per month, maybe paid ads management, a dashboard, and an account manager. The question is whether your business needs those. A single-location roofer usually doesn't; a five-location dental group might.

**One-time, $500 to $5,000.** Audits, profile setups, website rewrites. A profile setup at the low end should include category, services, service area, hours, photos, Bing and Apple, listings and a review system. A website rewrite at the high end should include new pages written for how customers search, a quote form, tracking and schema. Ask for the list of what's delivered on which day.

## What should every month's report show?

Five numbers, side by side with the month before and the day you started:

1. Calls from your Google profile.
2. Direction requests.
3. Website clicks from the profile.
4. Review count and average rating.
5. Where you rank on the map for your three most important searches, checked from a few spots in your service area, not just from the office.

If the report shows "impressions," "keywords tracked" or "pages optimized" and none of the five above, you're being shown activity instead of results. Impressions are how many times you appeared; calls are how many times someone chose you.

One more thing about the report: Google only keeps about six months of profile performance history. Whoever you hire should be saving it from day one, so that in month nine you can still see month one.

## What are the signs you're paying for a report instead of work?

You can't name a change made to your profile last month. Ask: what did you change, where, and can I see it? "We optimized your listing" is not an answer. "We changed your primary category to Roofing contractor on the 4th, added 12 photos and 6 services, and set holiday hours" is.

The contract is 12 months and you can't say why. Results take 60 to 90 days. A 12-month lock exists for the vendor's benefit. Month-to-month, or a 90-day evaluation, gets the same result with a way out.

You don't own the logins. Your Google profile, your website, your analytics and your listings should be in your name with the vendor added as a manager, removable in a minute. If the vendor "set up your Google profile" under their account, you don't own it, and leaving means starting over.

They guarantee rankings. Nobody controls Google's map results. A vendor can promise the work and the numbers; a vendor who promises "page one" or "number one" is either guessing or planning to show you a search nobody makes.

Reviews are being written for you. Fake reviews break Google's policy and federal rules, and when Google catches them it removes the reviews or the profile. If a vendor offers to "handle" reviews in a way that doesn't involve your real customers, walk away.

## How do I compare two quotes?

Put them side by side on four lines:

- What's done in month one, specifically, and what's done every month after.
- Which of the five numbers you'll see each month, and whether you'll see them next to the baseline.
- Who does the work, and how you reach them.
- How you leave, and what you keep when you do.

Price goes on the fifth line. The cheaper quote that answers the first four clearly usually beats the expensive one that doesn't.
